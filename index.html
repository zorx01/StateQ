The problem

Some places are too dangerous to send a person in first.

After a blast underground, inside a tank or a tunnel, or in a collapsed building, someone still has to go in and look before anyone else can. It is slow, it is often the riskiest part of the job, and everything else waits on it.

Simulated cave What the drone maps

How it works

  1. 01

    Hold back

    Everyone stays outside the danger zone, like a mine crew after a blast.

  2. 02

    The drone flies in

    It launches and flies through the space on its own. No GPS, no pilot.

  3. 03

    Maps it in 3D

    It keeps track of where it is while building a live LiDAR map of the space.

  4. 04

    Decide from safe ground

    The team reviews the map from outside and decides what is safe, sooner.

The drone

Small enough for tight spaces.

A compact 16-inch airframe, sized for mine headings and stopes, tunnels, tanks and ducts, carrying everything it needs to fly on its own.

Where it works

Wherever GPS and people can't safely go.

  • Underground mines

    Post-blast inspection and stope mapping, without sending a person in first.

  • Tunnels and underground civil

    Map and inspect tunnels under construction or in service.

  • Confined industrial spaces

    Tanks, silos, boilers and other enclosed plant spaces.

  • Infrastructure inspection

    Culverts, shafts and the undersides of bridges, where GPS drops out.

  • Search and rescue

    Look inside collapsed buildings and disaster sites before rescuers go in.

  • Defence

    Reconnaissance indoors and wherever GPS is jammed or denied.

About us

Drones that find their own way.

StateQ Dynamics builds autonomous drones that navigate and map without GPS, starting with underground mines. They go into dangerous, confined spaces in place of a person, so inspections are safer and work restarts sooner.

We are built inside the IIT Palakkad research ecosystem.
